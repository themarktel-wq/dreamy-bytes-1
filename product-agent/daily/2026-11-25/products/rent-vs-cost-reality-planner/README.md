# Rent vs Cost Reality Planner

An Excel / Google Sheets workbook for small landlords that answers one question: when taxes, insurance and running costs go up, which of my units need a rent review? For each unit it finds the break-even rent, tests the cost increases you choose, and shows the rent increase needed to reach your target monthly cash flow.

This is an operating-cost planner. It is not a purchase or ROI analyser: there is no purchase price, appreciation or loan-balance modelling.

## What you get
- Units sheet: up to 12 units (rent, mortgage P&I, yearly property tax, yearly insurance, other owner-paid monthly costs, maintenance reserve %, vacancy %)
- Scenario inputs: property tax increase %, insurance increase %, other-costs increase %, extra maintenance reserve points, target monthly cash flow per unit
- Break-even sheet: break-even rent today and under the scenario, monthly cash flow today and in the scenario, rent needed for your target, increase needed (amount and %), rent needed to hold today's cash flow, and a status per unit (OK / Below target / Losing money) with colour flags
- Portfolio summary: total cash flow today vs scenario, yearly change, units below target, units losing money, extra rent needed across the portfolio
- Rent Increase Table: portfolio cash flow and units below target at +0%, +3%, +5%, +7%, +10% (edit the percentages)
- Chart of current rent vs scenario break-even rent
- Sources and Notes sheet; works in Excel 2016+, Excel Online, Google Sheets and LibreOffice; no macros; any currency

## Good to know
The sample units and every starting number are examples, not forecasts or benchmarks; replace them with your own figures. Not modelled: income tax, depreciation, capital expenses, appreciation. Rent increases are limited or regulated in many places, so check local rules and your leases. This is a planning tool, not legal, tax or financial advice.

## Who it is for
Growing small investors and landlords (roughly 2 to 12 units) who want to know which units need a rent review before costs eat their cash flow.
