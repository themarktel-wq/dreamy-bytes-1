# Estimate vs Actual Variance Tracker

An Excel / Google Sheets workbook for movers who quote by the hour. After each job, log the estimated and actual hours and price. The workbook shows where your estimates systematically run short: variance per job and by job type, crew size, stairs and distance band, jobs flagged over your tolerance, and a suggested adjustment factor per group once you have enough jobs.

## What you get
- Settings: tolerance % for the Over/Under flag, minimum jobs before a suggestion is shown, distance band limits, summary year, an editable list of job types (up to 8) and crew sizes to analyse
- Jobs sheet (up to 80): date, reference, job type, crew size, estimated and actual hours, stairs (Yes/No), distance, estimated and final price; hours variance and %, price variance and %, an Over / Under / Within flag and distance band, calculated
- Variance Analysis: overall figures; tables by job type, crew size, stairs and distance band (jobs, estimated and actual hours, pooled variance %, average of job variances, share of jobs over tolerance, estimated and final price, suggested hours factor); a monthly summary; and a chart of variance by job type
- Sources and Notes sheet; works in Excel 2016+, Excel Online, Google Sheets and LibreOffice; no macros; any currency

## How to read it
Group variance % = (total actual hours - total estimated hours) / total estimated hours, so long jobs count for more than short ones. A suggested factor of 1.15 means actual hours ran about 15% above the estimate for that group. With only a few jobs the average is noisy, so the sheet shows "Need more jobs" until the minimum you set is reached.

## Good to know
The sample jobs and the 15% tolerance are examples. Some industry sources describe estimators as typically within roughly 10 to 15% in good conditions; that is context, not a rule for your business, so set your own tolerance. The tracker shows where estimates ran short, not why. Not tax, legal or financial advice.
